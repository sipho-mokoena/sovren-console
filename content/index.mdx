This site is the repository's documentation, rendered. It carries the
control-plane spec, the requirements index, the architecture decision records, the
glossary, the port map, and the runbook — and every one of those is the
repository's own file, read from where it already lives rather than copied into a
content folder.

## Where each thing comes from

| On this site                     | Read from                            |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Specification                    | `docs/specs/sovren-control-plane.md` |
| Requirements R1–R69              | `docs/tech-stack.md`                 |
| Decisions                        | every `docs/adr/*.md`                |
| Contributing                     | `docs/agents/*.md`                   |
| Port map                         | `config/ports.env`                   |
| Runbook, this page, the glossary | `content/*.mdx`                      |

The first four are globs over the repository, so an ADR written tomorrow appears
here tomorrow with nothing to add. The port map is read out of the registry while
the site builds. The last row is the small amount of prose the site owns.

## Where to start

If you have never seen this repository, read the
[specification](/docs/specs/sovren-control-plane) — it is authoritative on
behaviour and everything else defers to it — and then follow the
[runbook](/docs/guide/runbook) to get something running.

If you already know the shape of it and want the numbers,
[Requirements](/docs/tech-stack) is the index of R1 to R69, and the
[port map](/docs/guide/ports) says which port everything binds.

If you are about to write code, the [glossary](/docs/guide/glossary) is short and
the distinctions in it are the ones that are easy to lose.

## The two authorities

There are two documents, and they do not overlap.

The **specification** says what the system does. It is prose, it is
authoritative, and it is the thing to change when behaviour changes.

The **requirements index** says what has been asked for, numbered R1 to R69, and
records the stack decisions and the trade-offs behind them. It is the index, not
the argument; where the two disagree on behaviour, the specification wins.
